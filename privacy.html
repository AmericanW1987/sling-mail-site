Privacy policy

Last updated November 24, 2026

Sling-Mail is an email app for Windows and Android. It runs entirely on your own device. Sling-Mail has no servers of its own, no user accounts, no advertising and no analytics, and the developer never receives your email or your sign-in details.

What Sling-Mail accesses

  • Your email. Sling-Mail connects directly from your device to your email provider (for example Google or Microsoft) to download, show, organize, search and send your messages, only when you use the app.
  • Your sign-in. When you sign in with Google or Microsoft, you do it on their own page. Sling-Mail receives an access token that lets it read and send your mail, plus your email address. For other providers you enter your password in the app.

Where it is kept

Messages, settings, passwords and sign-in tokens are stored only on your device. Passwords and tokens are encrypted with your device's own protection (Windows Data Protection on PCs, the Android Keystore on phones). Nothing is copied to the developer or to any other server. If you use "Send to phone", your account settings travel from your PC to your phone encrypted, unlocked by a one-time code you type.

Google user data

Sling-Mail uses Google user data (your Gmail messages, labels and address, through the https://mail.google.com/ scope) only to provide the email features you use in the app: reading, organizing, searching, sending and notifying you about your mail. This data stays on your device. It is not sold, not used for advertising, not shared with anyone, and not used to develop, improve or train artificial intelligence or machine-learning models.

Sling-Mail's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Optional features that contact other services

  • AI assistant (off unless you set it up). If you add your own API key for Anthropic Claude or xAI Grok and ask the assistant to help with a message, the text you ask about is sent from your device to that provider to produce the answer, under your own agreement with them. Nothing is sent unless you ask.
  • Weather (dashboard). The location you type is looked up with Open-Meteo or the US Census geocoder, and forecasts come from the US National Weather Service (weather.gov).
  • Account setup. To find your mail servers, Sling-Mail looks up your email's domain name (never your address or password) in Mozilla's ISP database and public DNS (dns.google).
  • Updates. The app checks a GitHub repository for new versions.

Removing your data

Removing an account in Sling-Mail deletes its downloaded mail, settings and sign-in from that device; uninstalling the app removes everything. Your mail on your provider's servers is not touched. You can also revoke Sling-Mail's access at any time: for Google at myaccount.google.com/permissions, for Microsoft at account.live.com/consent/Manage.
